## The goal of this document

Help Derrick pick 5 names and send 5 personalized messages — in under 30 minutes. The criteria,
the archetype match, and the personalization angle are pre-done. Derrick just fills in the blanks.

---

## Who you're looking for (criteria refresher)

**Yes:** Overwhelmed founder, exec, or operator. Knows Derrick personally. Already believes AI
can help (not a skeptic who needs convincing). Will show up for a morning call at least 4x/week.
Will give honest feedback, not polite encouragement.

**No:** Investors who want to see the pitch deck. People who "will try it when they have time."
Anyone who'd be embarrassed by an Edge mistake. People whose company blocks Google OAuth.

**The question to ask yourself about each candidate:**
> "If Edge gave them bad advice on a call, would they text me about it — or just quietly stop using it?"

You want people who'd text you.

## The cohort (5 slots)

Pick one person per archetype slot. You don't need to fill all 5 immediately — starting with 2–3
and adding more gives you time to monitor the first experience before scaling.

---

### Slot 1 — The Recovering Founder (Type 1)

**Who:** Someone who ran a funded startup — exited, failed, or pivoted — and is now rebuilding
or doing something new. Understands burnout from the inside. Credible if they become a testimonial.

**Why they're valuable:** If Edge works for a recovering founder, it validates the core market.
Their testimonial is also the most powerful — "I've been through the thing" lends weight.

**Personalization angle for the message:**
> "[NAME], I've been building something I think would have been genuinely useful at [company/era].
> An AI that calls you every morning, tells you what to focus on, and reshapes your calendar around
> your actual priorities. Not a productivity app — more like a sharp EA who also knows your calendar.
> I'd love you to try it. Free, no commitment, takes 5 minutes to set up."

**Who to pick:** Someone in their 30s or 40s who ran something serious and you've stayed in touch
with. Not someone actively raising or in growth mode — they need headspace to engage.

### Slot 2 — The ADHD High-Performer (Type 3) ★ Priority

**Who:** Diagnosed or self-identified ADHD, succeeding despite the brain, but working harder than
they should have to. Founder, freelancer, or senior operator. Has tried many systems. Open about it.

**Why they're valuable:** Most loyal segment. Highest word-of-mouth velocity in tight-knit
ADHD communities. The product was designed for this person. If it clicks, they tell everyone.

**Personalization angle:**
> "[NAME], you know the problem I've been trying to solve — the calendar that constantly escapes
> you, the system that works for two weeks and then falls apart. I think I've actually built
> something that works differently this time. Calls you every morning, tells you what to do with
> your day, and actually applies it to your calendar. Want to be one of the first people to try it?
> No system to maintain."

**Who to pick:** Someone you've talked to about ADHD or productivity before. Not someone who will
be offended by the framing. Ideally someone who's said something like "I've tried everything."

**Name:** _______________________________________________

### Slot 3 — The Trusted Skeptic (Type 6) ★ Priority

**Who:** Experienced operator or founder, 38–50, has seen enough AI hype to be appropriately
skeptical, but trusts Derrick enough to try Edge anyway. Will give brutal, honest feedback.

**Why they're valuable:** If they become a convert, the testimonial is gold: "I didn't think
AI could actually do this." That's more powerful than any enthusiast testimonial. They'll also
find the bugs no one else would bother to report.

**Personalization angle:**
> "[NAME], I know you're not easily impressed by AI tools — which is exactly why I want you to try
> this one. It's not a chatbot. It calls you every morning and tells you where your week is going
> wrong. I want your honest read on whether it's real or not. Takes 5 minutes to set up. No cost.
> You'll probably find a dozen things wrong with it, and that's exactly what I need."

**Who to pick:** The most clear-eyed person in your network who you trust to give you the truth.
Someone who's told you hard things about your businesses before. The "I told you so" friend — in
the best way.

**Name:** _______________________________________________

### Slot 4 — Overwhelmed Operator or Executive (Type 2)

**Who:** Director or VP at a fast-moving company. 4–6 hours of meetings per day. Priorities feel
blurry. Wants more control over their calendar but doesn't have it.

**Why they're valuable:** The largest segment of the long-term ICP. If Edge works for a senior IC
or director, not just a founder, the market is much bigger.

**Personalization angle:**
> "[NAME], I've been building something I think you'd get a lot out of given how packed your
> weeks have gotten. An AI that calls you every morning, tells you what matters today, and
> actually moves things on your calendar. No app to maintain — just a call. Want to be one of
> the first to try it? Takes about 5 minutes to set up."

**Who to pick:** Someone at a company where IT policy won't block Google OAuth. Avoid people in
large enterprises with locked-down IT. Prefer growth-stage startup executives.

## The send sequence

Once you have names:

**Week 1:**
1. Send Slot 2 (ADHD High-Performer) and Slot 3 (Trusted Skeptic) first — they're the most
   valuable, and you want the most time to monitor their experience.
2. Send Slot 1 (Recovering Founder) 2–3 days later.

**Week 2 (after first user has had a successful first call):**
3. Send Slots 4 and 5.

**Why stagger?** Each user deserves your full attention in week 1. Adding all 5 simultaneously
means you can't catch problems before they affect the next person. One excellent experience is
worth more than five mediocre ones.

---

## Before you send anything

- [ ] Twilio is live (you can make a real call)
- [ ] You've completed the onboarding flow yourself with a fresh account
- [ ] The activation moment works (shows real calendar data)
- [ ] You've had one test call that sounded right

If any of those aren't true: don't send. Fix first.

---

## After you send

- If they say yes within 24 hours: send the link + a short personal note immediately.
- If no response in 5 days: one follow-up (template in `content/design-partner-outreach.md`).
- If no response after follow-up: move on. Don't chase.

You want people who say "yes" quickly. Slow or reluctant yes = low retention = bad signal.

---

## What NOT to do

- **Don't send to all 5 at once** — you can't support them all in week 1
- **Don't send to people you're not close with** — trust has to exist before the ask
- **Don't send until Twilio is live** — promising a morning call and having it fail is worse
  than waiting
- **Don't pitch the product** — you're inviting a friend to try something you built
