# Edge — Live Demo Script + Pitch One-Pager
_5-minute live demo flow for high-performers. What to show, in order, to produce
"I need this." Also: the written one-page pitch. Last updated: June 18, 2026._

---

## Part 1 — The 5-Minute Live Demo

### The goal

By the end of 5 minutes, the person should:
1. Understand what Edge does (it calls you, it reads your calendar, it takes action)
2. Have seen something specific and true about a real calendar
3. Have felt the emotional weight of "nobody's ever shown me this about my own week"
4. Ask "how do I get access?"

If they're asking questions, you're winning. If they're nodding politely, you've lost them.

---

### What you need

- Your own Edge dashboard open on a laptop or screen share
- Your actual calendar visible — real events, real names (or a demo account with realistic data)
- The Edge Score card visible in the top section
- The Activity tab and "What Edge knows" tab accessible
- A phone nearby in case you want to trigger a live call (optional)

**Do not use a mock or prototype.** The power of the demo is that it's real. If the calendar
shows fake placeholder events, you've immediately undercut the trust claim.

---

### The flow (5 minutes)

---

#### Opening — the one question (30 seconds)

Before you open the laptop, ask:

> "Can I ask — when you sat down to start work this week, how long did it take you to figure
> out what to actually work on?"

Wait for the answer. Don't fill the silence. Then:

> "Most people I talk to say it takes 20, 30 minutes. Some say they never really figure it out
> — they just start reacting. Edge is the answer to that problem. Let me show you."

_[Open the laptop. Navigate to the Edge dashboard.]_

---

#### Scene 1 — The Edge Score (1 minute)

_[Show the Edge Score card on the dashboard home tab.]_

> "This is the Edge Score. It's a number from 0 to 100 that represents how well your week is
> structured for what you're trying to accomplish. Four components: Focus — how much of your
> calendar time goes toward your stated priorities. Energy — your recovery level. Clarity — how
> much Edge knows about you. Momentum — whether you're following through."

_[Point to the current score and its components.]_

> "Right now mine is [N]. That means [honest read: 'my Focus is high but my Energy is
> calibrating because I haven't had a great week of sleep' or whatever is true]. The number is
> real — it's derived from my actual calendar and Whoop data."

_[Pause for a beat.]_

> "The goal of every morning call is to raise this number."

---

#### Scene 2 — The hero loop (90 seconds)

_[Show the hero loop card on the dashboard — the "Improve my day" or diagnosis card.]_

> "Every morning, Edge analyzes my schedule and proposes changes. This is what it's seeing right
> now."

_[Read the diagnosis lines aloud — e.g., "No time blocked for your top priority this week."
or "Three back-to-back meetings Wednesday afternoon with no breaks."]_

> "These aren't invented. They come from what's actually on my calendar and what I've told Edge
> matters to me."

_[Click Apply — or describe what happens when you do.]_

> "If I click Apply, Edge will execute the whole plan — create the focus block, buffer the
> back-to-back meetings, whatever it's proposed. Every change is logged in the Activity tab.
> If I don't like any of it, one click undoes the whole thing."

_[Show the Activity tab briefly — the log of Edge actions.]_

> "Everything Edge does is visible here. Nothing is hidden."

---

#### Scene 3 — What Edge knows (45 seconds)

_[Navigate to "What Edge knows" tab.]_

> "After every call, Edge stores what it learned. Goals, context, people I've mentioned,
> patterns it's noticed. This is my profile — what Edge knows about me."

_[Scroll through a few fact categories briefly.]_

> "I can edit or delete anything here. If Edge got something wrong, I fix it in one click.
> This is what 'AI you can trust' actually looks like — not a black box, but a file I can
> read and correct."

---

#### Scene 4 — The morning call (1 minute)

_[Show a recent call transcript or a call summary from the dashboard. Or, if demo context
allows, play a short clip of a real call.]_

> "Every morning at [time], Edge calls my phone. Here's what a call looks like."

_[Read or show the call summary — what Edge said, what was proposed, what was done.]_

_Or if doing a live call:_

> "Actually — do you want to hear what it sounds like? I'll trigger a test right now."

_[Trigger the call via Vapi dashboard. Let the demo subject hear the first 30–45 seconds of
how Edge opens a call.]_

> "That's it. 5–10 minutes. Every morning. Edge opens with something true about your week —
> not a generic 'how are you,' but a specific observation from your calendar — and it moves
> from there."

---

#### Close — the question that closes (30 seconds)

> "Most of the people I've shown this to have one of two reactions. Either: 'I've been doing
> fine without this.' Or: 'I didn't know what I was missing until just now.'"

_[Pause. Let them respond.]_

If they're interested: "I'm bringing in a small group of design partners — the first 5 people
who'll use it seriously and tell me what's wrong with it. Would you want to be one of them?"

If they're skeptical: "That's fair. What would have to be true for something like this to be
worth your time?"

---

### What NOT to do

- **Don't pitch features.** Show what's there, let them ask.
- **Don't apologize for what's missing.** "We don't have X yet" is a confidence killer.
- **Don't rush through the Clarity reveal.** That's the moment. Let it breathe.
- **Don't use placeholder data.** If your calendar is sparse, use a test account with realistic events, not fake ones.
- **Don't explain how it works technically.** Nobody cares that it's Vapi + Claude. Show what it does.

---

### Objection handling in the demo

**"Isn't this just like Motion/Reclaim?"**
> "Those tools automate when things get scheduled. Edge helps you decide what matters — then
> calls you every morning and does it. Different category."

**"I don't want AI having access to my calendar."**
> "Completely fair. Everything Edge does is visible in the Activity tab — you can see every
> action it took. And you can disconnect your calendar at any time from Settings. Nothing is
> hidden, nothing is permanent."

**"I'm too busy to add another tool."**
> "That's the wrong frame — Edge is replacing your morning planning time, not adding to it.
> Instead of spending 20 minutes figuring out what to work on, Edge tells you in 5."

## Part 2 — The Written One-Page Pitch

_For sending after the demo, or when someone asks "can you send me something?"_

### Edge — One Page

**What it is:**
Edge is an AI chief of staff that calls you every morning. In 5–10 minutes, it reads your
calendar, tells you what to focus on, and reshapes your week around what actually matters.

**The problem it solves:**
Most high-performers aren't failing because they're working too little. They're failing because
they're working on the wrong things, in the wrong order, at the wrong energy level. Their
calendar fills up reactively — meetings, requests, inbound — and their actual priorities get
squeezed out.

Edge exists to fix that. Not as another app you have to check. As a call that comes to you.

---

**How it works:**

1. **Connect your calendar.** Edge reads your events, understands your schedule, and identifies
   the gaps between what's on your calendar and what you say matters.

2. **Tell Edge your priorities** (or let it derive them from your calendar history — it can
   infer what you've been spending time on for the past 6 months).

3. **Edge calls you every morning.** It opens with your Edge Score — a 0–100 number reflecting
   how well your schedule maps to your priorities — and proposes specific changes. You approve.
   It executes.

4. **Edge remembers.** Every call, it learns more about you — your constraints, your goals, your
   patterns. The calls get sharper over time.

---

**What makes it different:**

- **It calls you.** No app to remember to open. Edge comes to you.
- **It takes action.** Not suggestions — actual calendar changes, with your approval.
- **It shows its work.** Every action is logged. Every fact it's stored is visible and editable.
  Nothing is a black box.
- **It knows your energy.** Integrates with Whoop to factor in your recovery before recommending
  anything demanding.

---

**The trust model:**

Most AI tools hide their reasoning. Edge does the opposite. Your Activity tab shows every change
Edge made and why. Your "What Edge knows" tab shows every fact it has stored. You can edit or
delete anything. Account deletion removes every record immediately.

This isn't a marketing claim. It's the architecture.

---

**Who it's for:**

Founders, senior operators, and ambitious high-performers who:
- Feel like their calendar controls them, not the other way around
- Have tried productivity tools and eventually stopped using them
- Know what they *should* be doing but can't consistently *do* it
- Are dealing with burnout, energy depletion, or decision fatigue
