# Angel Outreach Strategy
_For Derrick — how to convert warm relationships into introductions to angel investors, and how to close first checks._
_Run this in parallel with the beta, not after it. The fundraising narrative starts building now._

---

## The premise

You don't need a deck to start raising. You need a story, a signal, and a warm intro. You have all three:
- **Story:** burnout-prevention AI built by someone who burned out building a $100M company
- **Signal:** 5 design partners in active beta, daily calls, Edge Score improving
- **Warm intros:** every advisor you have knows at least one angel who backs early-stage AI

The goal of this strategy is not to close a round. It's to build a pipeline of 10–15 warm conversations by end of July so that when you're ready to raise (August–September, after beta retention data), you have investors who already know the story.

---

## Step 1 — Update your LinkedIn and email signature (before anything else)

Before you approach anyone, they will Google you. Your LinkedIn needs to say what you're building in one line, not your last company. Update:

**Headline:**
> "Building Edg3 — AI that helps high-performers stay focused and avoid burnout | Founder, ex-[company]"

**About section (first 2 lines — what shows before "see more"):**
> "After helping build a $100M company, I burned out. Not because of the work — because I had no system for staying aligned with what actually mattered. Edg3 is that system."

Do this first. It takes 10 minutes and it makes every subsequent conversation warmer.

---

## Step 2 — The advisor warmup (Week 1)

Send a brief personal update to every advisor. Not a pitch — an honest update. Use the template below.

**Template — Advisor warmup email:**

Subject: Quick update from the Edg3 build

> Hi [name],
>
> Wanted to drop a quick note — we launched our closed beta this week with our first 5 design partners. Daily morning calls, priority derivation, the whole thing. Early results are promising; day-7 retention is strong so far.
>
> I've been thinking about the path to August and what the fundraising setup needs to look like by then. I'd love to grab 15 minutes in the next few weeks, even just to get your honest read on the story we're telling.
>
> Let me know if you have a window — [Calendly or "just reply with a time that works"].
>
> Derrick

**Why this works:** it's not asking for anything specific. It signals momentum, invites a conversation, and lets you ask the real question in person: "who in your network is investing in early-stage AI right now?"

---

## Step 3 — The intro request (Week 3–4, after the advisor call)

After the call, you'll have a feel for who's engaged. For the ones who seem genuinely excited, ask directly:

**Template — Intro request (send after the advisor call):**

> "One thing I meant to ask on the call — are there one or two people in your network who actively invest in early-stage AI, or founders who've been thinking about burnout and focus as a problem space? I'm not looking for a warm body with a checkbook; I want people who'd find the problem genuinely interesting. If anyone comes to mind, even a loose intro would mean a lot."

This language is non-aggressive and gives the advisor a graceful filter ("I'm not looking for anyone random"). Most good advisors will think of 1–2 names immediately.

**Follow-up if they offer an intro:**
> "That would be great — would it be easiest for me to send you a one-liner you can forward, or would you rather make an email intro directly? Either works."

If they offer a one-liner forward, send them this:

---

**Forwardable one-liner (for advisors to use):**
> "I've been watching Derrick Fung build Edg3 — it's a voice AI that gives high-performers a 10-minute morning brief tied to their calendar and priorities, like an executive coach who actually knows your week. He's in early beta with 5 design partners and looking to have a few conversations with early-stage AI investors. Worth a quick call if that's in your wheelhouse."

---

## Step 4 — The first investor call

**Goal:** not to pitch, to connect. You want them to say "this is interesting, tell me more" — that converts to a follow-up where you can talk terms.

**Opening:**
> "Thanks for taking the time — [advisor name] mentioned you've been active in the AI space. I figured even 20 minutes would be worth it to get your honest read on what we're building."

**The 2-minute story:**
> "I burned out building my last company. Not dramatically — just slowly ran out of energy while still going through the motions. When I looked back at what went wrong, it came down to this: I never had a system that kept me connected to what actually mattered on a day-to-day basis."
>
> "Edg3 is that system. It's a voice AI that calls you every morning, briefs you on your day, and pushes you to stay connected to your priorities — not just react to what's in your calendar. We're in beta with 5 design partners right now. Day-7 retention is [X]."

Then stop. Let them respond. Most investors will ask a question that tells you exactly what they care about.

**Common questions + honest answers:**

*"What's the monetization model?"*
> "$199/month for individuals. We're watching whether teams start appearing — a few design partners have already mentioned sharing it with their chiefs of staff. That's a natural enterprise expansion, but we're not building for it yet."

*"Why AI voice specifically?"*
> "Text-based tools require you to open an app and remember to check in. A call meets you where you are. The people who actually benefit from this are the ones who won't sit down and fill out a morning journal — they're already in motion. Voice removes that friction entirely."

*"What's the defensibility?"*
> "Short-term: the personalization loop. Every call makes the next one more accurate — Edge learns your language, your priorities, your patterns. Long-term: the behavioral data set. We'll have longitudinal data on what focus and energy patterns predict performance for high-performers. That's not something you can replicate without years of calls."

*"What are you raising?"*
> "I'm in conversations now, not running a formal process yet. I want to close a seed round — probably $500K–$1M — in August/September once we have 30-day retention data from the beta. I'd rather talk to people now so the relationship exists when we're ready."

## Step 5 — The pipeline tracker

Keep a simple table. The goal is 10–15 conversations by end of July, not checks today.

| Name | How connected | Source | Status | Last contact | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Name] | Warm (advisor intro) | [advisor] | Scheduled | [date] | Interested in AI productivity |
| [Name] | Cold (LinkedIn) | Self | Reached out | [date] | — |

**Stages:** `identified` → `intro requested` → `intro made` → `call scheduled` → `call done` → `follow-up` → `passed` / `interested` / `committed`

---

## Timing the raise

| Milestone | Target date | What it unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| 5 design partners, day-7 calls | June 17–30 | "We're in beta" story |
| First positive testimonial | Week 2–3 of beta | Social proof for conversations |
| 30-day retention data (3+ users) | End of July | Hard retention numbers ("X% still taking daily calls at 30 days") |
| $50K ARR equivalent (waitlist) | Aug–Sept | Revenue multiple story, not just users |

**Start conversations now. Close checks when you have the 30-day data.** The right angels will wait 6–8 weeks for retention if they believe the story. Rushing before you have the data is usually worse — you'll get low valuations and investors who don't understand the product.

---

## What you're NOT raising on

Don't lead with TAM ("the burnout market is $X billion"). Sophisticated angels know that's noise. Lead with the problem, the personal story, and the retention signal. The TAM is implicit — if you can retain 5 founders on daily calls, you can retain 5,000.

Don't apologize for being pre-revenue. You're pre-launch by design. The design partner model is the right call — you're being intentional.
