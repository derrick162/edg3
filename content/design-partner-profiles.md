## What makes a good design partner

A design partner is not a beta tester. They're a collaborator. The bar is higher:

- **Uses it daily** — shows up for the morning call consistently enough to generate real signal
- **Gives honest feedback** — doesn't soften criticism out of politeness; will tell you when something is broken or annoying
- **Fits the ICP** — genuinely overwhelmed high-performer; calendar-driven; already believes AI can help
- **Has 10 minutes a month** for a check-in call with Derrick
- **Trusts Derrick personally** — warm relationship means faster, more honest feedback

**Who to avoid:**
- People who will "try it when they get a chance" — low commitment = no signal
- People who will be overly positive — nice but useless
- People who want to invest or get a job — conflicting incentives
- Anyone who'd be embarrassed if Edge made a mistake (high public profiles with fragile image)

## Candidate types (7 archetypes)

### Type 1 — The Recovering Founder
Someone who's been through a high-pressure startup and is rebuilding. They understand burnout from the inside. They have the credibility to be a strong testimonial if Edge works. They're honest about when things don't.

**Why they're valuable:** founder-market fit validation. If Edge works for this person, it works for the ICP.

**What they look like:** 30s-40s, previously ran a funded startup, now rebuilding or doing something new. Not currently in a hypergrowth phase — recovering or restarting.

**Risk:** may have too much self-awareness to rely on a tool; may be emotionally protective about admitting they need help.

---

### Type 2 — The Overwhelmed Operator
A senior IC, director, or VP at a fast-moving company. Calendar is full of meetings. Priorities feel unclear. Constantly reactive. Not a founder — doesn't have the luxury of restructuring their schedule — but desperately wants to.

**Why they're valuable:** they're the largest segment of the ICP. If Edge works for them, you have a scalable market.

**What they look like:** director/VP at a tech company, 5–10 reports, 4–6 hours of meetings per day, hasn't had a protected focus block in weeks.

**Risk:** company IT policies may block Google OAuth or Whoop integrations. Check before onboarding.

---

### Type 3 — The ADHD High-Performer
Someone with diagnosed or self-identified ADHD who is succeeding despite it — but working 2x as hard to compensate. Time blindness, decision fatigue, and context-switching cost are real problems for them.

**Why they're valuable:** most loyal segment. If Edge removes friction, they tell everyone. ADHD communities are tight-knit and vocal. Word-of-mouth velocity is highest here.

**What they look like:** founder, freelancer, or senior operator. Has tried every productivity system and they've all eventually failed. Knows their brain works differently. Open about it.

**Risk:** onboarding needs to be frictionless — high ADHD users won't push through a rough setup. The first call must be good or they won't come back.

---

### Type 4 — The Ambitious Optimist
Someone in their late 20s/early 30s building their career aggressively. Not burned out yet — but heading there. Wants tools that make them more effective and sees AI as a genuine competitive advantage.

**Why they're valuable:** they grow into the core ICP. Early adoption by this segment creates long-term retention and referral density.

**What they look like:** startup employee, consultant, or early-stage founder. High output. Not yet cynical about productivity tools. Willing to experiment.

**Risk:** lower urgency than other archetypes — may use it inconsistently until they hit a wall.

---

### Type 5 — The Whole-Life Operator
Someone whose priorities span professional AND personal meaningfully. Running a business AND training for something AND managing a family AND dealing with financial complexity. Edge's ability to see the whole person, not just the work calendar, is the differentiator for them.

**Why they're valuable:** validates that Edge works beyond the pure work-productivity frame. Opens the "life OS" positioning.

**What they look like:** 35–45, founder or senior executive, also training (running, gym, sport), family or relationship commitments, financial complexity (investments, real estate, etc.).

**Risk:** calendar complexity may expose Edge bugs — good for learning, bad if they're the first user and the experience is rough.

---

### Type 6 — The Trusted Skeptic
Someone who has tried AI tools before, been disappointed, and is now skeptical — but trusts Derrick enough to try Edge anyway. Their bar is higher, which means their positive feedback is worth more.

**Why they're valuable:** if they become a convert, they're the most credible testimonial. "I didn't think AI could do this" is more powerful than "I love AI tools."

**What they look like:** experienced operator or founder, 38–50, has seen enough hype cycles to be appropriately cynical. Honest to a fault. Will give you the brutal feedback others won't.

**Risk:** higher bar to clear. If Edge disappoints in the first week, they may not give a second chance.

---

### Type 7 — The Connector
Someone with a large network in the ICP. If they love Edge, they send 10 people. Their value is partly as a user and partly as a distribution node.

**Why they're valuable:** one positive design partner here = a wave of warm referrals.

**What they look like:** investor, podcast host, community organizer, or well-networked operator with 5,000+ LinkedIn followers in the target demographic.

**Risk:** may not use the product deeply enough to give strong feedback. Treat as a hybrid: user + GTM partner.

---

## Recommended first cohort composition

For the first 5 design partners, aim for:
- 1 × Type 1 (Recovering Founder) — ICP validation
- 1 × Type 3 (ADHD High-Performer) — word-of-mouth velocity
- 1 × Type 6 (Trusted Skeptic) — honest feedback + credible testimonial
- 2 × Type 2 or 5 (Operator or Whole-Life) — breadth signal

Avoid loading the first cohort with Type 7 (Connectors) — optimize for feedback quality before referral volume.

---

## What to ask them at onboarding

1. "What's your biggest frustration with how your week usually goes?"
2. "Have you tried other AI tools? What happened?"
3. "What would make you feel like Edge was actually working?"

Their answers anchor what to listen for in week 1 feedback.
