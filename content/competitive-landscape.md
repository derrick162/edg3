# Edge — Competitive Landscape
_Who else touches calendar/AI-EA/burnout, and how Edge is different._
_Last updated: June 18, 2026._

---

## The market landscape

Edge sits at the intersection of three categories:
1. **AI calendar / scheduling** — tools that automate when things happen
2. **AI executive assistants** — tools that help with knowledge work broadly
3. **Burnout / energy management** — tools that monitor and protect personal capacity

No existing tool owns all three. Edge's bet is that the intersection — voice-native, energy-aware,
calendar-native AI that proactively contacts you — is a new category worth owning.

---

## Direct competitors (calendar AI)

### Reclaim.ai
**What it does:** Automatically protects focus time, buffers, habits, and tasks on your calendar
based on rules you set. Integrates with Asana, Linear, Todoist.

**Users:** Knowledge workers who want calendar automation without manual scheduling.

**Why it fails for Edge's ICP:**
- Rule-based, not intent-based: it optimizes *when* to schedule things, not *what* to work on
- No reasoning about priorities — it protects time, but doesn't know what that time should be for
- No voice, no call — still requires you to open an app and maintain configuration
- No energy layer — Whoop integration doesn't exist; burnout is not in the product model
- Calendar is the output, not the input: users still tell it what to protect

**Derrick's answer:** "Reclaim is great at protecting focus time once you've told it what to
protect. Edge tells you what to protect, then protects it."

**Price:** $10–$20/month. This anchor makes Edge's pricing ($49–$79/month) a signal of category
difference — not just a more expensive version of Reclaim.

---

### Motion
**What it does:** AI-powered daily planner. Automatically schedules tasks and meetings, builds
optimal daily schedules, and reschedules when things shift.

**Users:** Individual contributors and small teams who want automated daily planning.

**Why it fails for Edge's ICP:**
- Overly prescriptive: users report the AI feeling like it's managing them, not working for them
- Task-entry required: still requires you to feed it todos and deadlines
- No voice call: purely app-based; high abandonment rates in week 2–3 once novelty wears off
- No energy layer, no morning ritual, no human connection
- Founders and executives specifically struggle with it — it's optimized for people with defined
  task lists, not people navigating ambiguous strategic work

**Derrick's answer:** "Motion tells you when to do tasks. Edge helps you figure out which tasks
matter. And it calls you — you don't have to remember to open it."

**Price:** $34–$49/month. Closer to Edge's territory, but still in the "automation" frame.

---

### Clockwise
**What it does:** AI that finds the best times for meetings, protects focus time, and optimizes
team schedules.

**Users:** Teams trying to reduce back-to-back meetings and protect deep work windows.

**Why it fails for Edge's ICP:**
- Primarily a team/calendar optimization tool — solves scheduling coordination, not individual focus
- No personal AI layer — no conversation, no reasoning about priorities
- No voice, no proactive contact
- The burnout/energy frame is absent

**Derrick's answer:** "Clockwise is for scheduling meetings better. Edge is for deciding what to
do with the time that's left."

## Adjacent competitors (AI EA / personal AI)

### Notion AI
**What it does:** AI embedded into Notion for writing, summarizing, and organizing knowledge.

**Why not a direct competitor:**
- Document-first, not calendar-first
- No proactive contact — you go to Notion, it doesn't come to you
- No Google Calendar integration (as a core feature)
- Positioned as productivity + knowledge management, not focus + energy

**Where they could compete:** If Notion added a "daily briefing" feature tied to calendar
integrations, they'd be in the same conversation. They haven't, and their product DNA is not
about proactive AI agents.

---

### Microsoft 365 Copilot / Google Duet AI
**What it does:** AI embedded into existing office suites — email drafting, meeting summaries,
calendar suggestions.

**Why not a direct competitor (for now):**
- Reactive, not proactive — only acts when you ask
- No reasoning about priorities or energy
- Optimized for enterprise, not high-performing individuals
- No morning call ritual; no burnout framing

**Long-term risk:** Microsoft or Google could build Edge's feature set with a fraction of the
trust work Edge is doing. However, their incentive is to keep you in their ecosystem (Teams,
Outlook), not to challenge your calendar structure. They're unlikely to build a product that
tells you which meetings to cancel.

---

### Clara, x.ai (defunct / acquired)
**What they did:** Email-based AI scheduling assistants that handled meeting booking.

**Why they failed:** Solved one narrow problem (scheduling coordination) without touching the
harder problem (prioritization and energy management). Also email-based, which feels slow and
transactional compared to voice.

**Lesson:** Narrow scheduling automation isn't enough. Users want judgment, not just execution.

## Energy / burnout tools

### Whoop
**What it does:** Wearable that tracks recovery, sleep, and strain. Provides a daily readiness
score.

**How Edge uses it:** Edge reads Whoop's recovery score (with user permission) and integrates it
into the morning briefing and hero loop recommendations.

**Why they're not a competitor:** Whoop gives data but no action. It tells you your recovery is
low; it doesn't tell you which meeting to cancel because of it. Edge is the action layer on top
of Whoop's data.

**The partnership angle:** Edge + Whoop is a natural pairing for the Whole-Life Operator ICP.

---

### Oura
Same analysis as Whoop — data without action. Edge could integrate Oura in a future release.

---

### Calm, Headspace, Nap pods, etc.
Not competitors — these are recovery tools, not productivity tools. They're complements to Edge
for users dealing with burnout.

## Human alternatives

### Executive assistant (EA)
**What a good EA does:** Manages calendar, handles scheduling, filters email, prepares briefings,
follows up on commitments, books travel, handles admin.

**How Edge compares:**
- Cost: EA = $40,000–$120,000/year. Edge = $49–79/month (~$600–950/year). 50–100x cheaper.
- Coverage: EA works 9–5. Edge calls at 7 AM, tracks patterns across months, never takes PTO.
- Scope: EA handles logistics. Edge handles strategic focus — which is the harder problem.
- Trust: EA builds trust through relationship. Edge builds trust through transparency (show your work).
- Limitation: Edge cannot schedule calls with a human counterpart, handle travel, or do admin.
  An EA and Edge are complementary for executives who can afford both.

**The positioning:** Edge is not trying to replace EAs. It's trying to be an EA for the 99% of
founders and senior operators who can't afford one.

---

### Business coach / advisor
**What they do:** Help founders think through priorities, accountability, strategy.

**How Edge compares:**
- Cost: $200–$500/hour for a good business coach. Edge is 24/7 at $79/month.
- Coverage: A coach talks to you once a week. Edge talks to you every morning.
- Accountability: Similar — the morning call creates a daily commitment loop similar to coaching.
- Limitation: A coach has lived experience and can give guidance Edge cannot. Edge complements,
  doesn't replace.

## Where Edge wins

| Dimension | Why Edge wins |
|---|---|
| **Proactive contact** | Edge calls you. No other tool in this space does. Pull vs. push is the single biggest advantage. |
| **Voice-native** | Voice captures commitment in a way typing doesn't. Edge works at 7 AM when you're not ready to open an app. |
| **Energy-aware** | No calendar tool integrates health/recovery data into recommendations. Edge does. |
| **Trust + transparency** | Activity log, fact correction, full account deletion. Users can audit everything. |
| **Priority-first** | Edge starts with what matters and works backward to the calendar. Others start with the calendar. |
| **Burnout-conscious** | Edge will push back on over-scheduling. No automation tool does this. |

---

## Where Edge is vulnerable

| Risk | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Google / Microsoft build this** | They could. Their incentives work against it (they want more meetings, not fewer). But this is a real long-term risk. |
| **Vapi dependency** | If Vapi prices up, changes the API, or gets acquired, the core call mechanic is at risk. Mitigation: build strong enough user retention that we can afford to pay more, and monitor alternatives. |
| **Google OAuth velocity** | Without verification, growth is capped at ~100 test users. This is the #1 near-term ceiling. |
| **Habit formation** | Daily habit products fail in week 2–3 if they don't deliver enough value fast enough. Edge's countermeasure: the morning call comes to you, reducing activation energy. |
| **Trust is slow to build** | The trust wedge is real, but trust takes time. Users need multiple accurate, helpful calls before they fully believe Edge. The first week is fragile. |
