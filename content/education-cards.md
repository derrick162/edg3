## 7. How to build your calendar around your energy

**Preview:** Green, yellow, red — a simple playbook for matching every task to the right moment.

**Body:**
Not all tasks cost the same. An important client meeting, a strategic decision, a deep work session — these draw from a different reservoir than clearing your inbox or booking a flight. The mistake most people make is treating their calendar like a flat surface, dropping tasks wherever there's space, regardless of what that hour actually has to offer.

Here's a simple framework Edge uses:

**Green days and windows** are your highest-capacity moments — when sleep was good, recovery is strong, and you're firing on all cylinders. This is where your most demanding, highest-value work belongs: external meetings, strategic planning, deep creative work, hard decisions, important conversations. Exercise fits here too — your body is ready for it. Don't waste green on email.

**Yellow** is your moderate-energy state — capable and functional, but not at your peak. Medium-stakes work goes here: internal syncs, writing first drafts, reviewing and responding, planning and organizing. Steady, meaningful progress without burning your best fuel.

**Red** is your lowest-capacity window — low cognitive load only. Email, admin, routine tasks, errands, passive reading. Not because these don't matter, but because they don't require your best. Save your best for when you have it.

Edge tracks your energy daily — through Whoop, your morning check-in, or how you sound on the call — and automatically reshapes your calendar to match. High-demand blocks move to green windows. Admin gets pushed to the dip. Your day gets easier without you having to think about it.

## 1. Manage your calendar by your energy

**Preview:** Your calendar shouldn't just tell you *what* to do — it should tell you *when* to do it.

**Body:**
Most people schedule by availability. If there's a free slot, the task goes there. But not all hours are created equal.

Your energy has a rhythm. There are windows in the day when you're sharp, creative, and capable of your best thinking — and windows when you're better off on autopilot. Ignoring that rhythm doesn't make you more productive. It just means you're grinding through your hardest work at your lowest capacity.

Edge learns your energy pattern — when you peak, when you dip, when you need to protect your focus — and organizes your calendar around it. High-energy tasks in high-energy windows. Admin and routine work in the dips. Recovery protected, not squeezed out.

The result isn't just a better calendar. It's a day that actually feels manageable.

## 2. What burnout really is — and how Edge protects you from it

**Preview:** Burnout doesn't happen overnight. It accumulates — and it's preventable.

**Body:**
Burnout isn't a dramatic collapse. It's a slow drain. It happens when you consistently spend more energy than you recover — when every week is a little more output, a little less rest, a little more reactive, a little less intentional.

High performers are especially vulnerable because the early warning signs feel like success. Busy = productive. Tired = hard-working. Full calendar = in demand.

Edge is designed to catch the pattern before it catches you. When your Energy score dips for several days running, Edge notices. When your calendar is packed with high-demand work on a low-recovery day, Edge says something. When you haven't touched a focus area in a week, Edge surfaces it.

Think of it as a chief of staff who pays attention to your energy the way a good coach pays attention to your training load — always asking: is this sustainable?

## 3. Your Edge Score, explained

**Preview:** One number. Four things that determine how ready you are to do your best work.

**Body:**
Your Edge Score is a daily readout of how set up you are to perform — not how busy you are, but how well-positioned you are. It's made up of four components:

**Focus (30%)** — Does your calendar reflect your top three priorities? A high Focus score means the things that matter most have protected time. A low score means your calendar is full of everything except the work that moves the needle.

**Energy (30%)** — How's your capacity today? Informed by your Whoop data (sleep + recovery) or your self-reported check-in. Edge uses this to know whether to push or protect.

**Clarity (20%)** — How well does Edge know you? The more you share — your focus areas, energy profile, connected tools — the smarter Edge gets. Clarity grows as Edge learns.

**Momentum (20%)** — Are you showing up consistently? Daily calls completed, focus confirmed, plans followed through. Momentum is the compounding edge. Small, consistent actions build the score — and the results.

The goal isn't a perfect 100. It's a score that's honest, improving, and working for you.

## 4. Why just three areas of focus?

**Preview:** The cost of too many priorities is that nothing gets prioritized.

**Body:**
When everything is important, nothing is. It sounds like a cliché because it's true — and most high performers have felt it.

The problem with a long list of priorities is that it doesn't force a decision. It just postpones one. You end up spreading attention across eight things and making meaningful progress on none of them.

Three focus areas is the constraint that makes the decision real. Which three things, if you moved them forward this week, would matter most? Not which ten things are important — which three deserve your calendar.

Edge is built around this constraint deliberately. The Focus score, the morning briefing, the calendar reshaping — all of it is organized around your three areas. Not because three is a magic number, but because limits create clarity. And clarity is what lets you actually execute.

Change them whenever you need to. But pick three.

## 5. The science of sleep and recovery

**Preview:** Sleep isn't rest. It's the work your body does so you can do the work you want to.

**Body:**
Recovery isn't passive. While you sleep, your brain consolidates memory, clears metabolic waste, regulates mood, and repairs tissue. Whoop measures this process directly — your recovery score reflects how well that work actually got done.

A high recovery score means your body is ready. A low one means it's still catching up — and pushing hard into a low-recovery day costs more than it produces.

Edge uses your recovery data to adapt your day. On green days, it protects your peak windows for your hardest focus work. On red days, it suggests lighter loads and reminds you that protecting today's recovery is an investment in tomorrow's output.

This is what separates sustainable performance from the cycle most high performers know too well: push hard, crash, recover, push again. Edge tries to flatten that curve — not by doing less, but by doing the right things at the right time.

## 6. Working with your peaks and troughs

**Preview:** You already have a rhythm. Edge just helps you use it.

**Body:**
Most people have a natural performance curve across the day — a window of peak alertness and creative capacity, usually in the morning or early afternoon, followed by a dip, and sometimes a secondary lift later in the day.

Research on circadian rhythms and cognitive performance consistently shows the same thing: the work you do in your peak window is qualitatively different from the same work done in a trough. Your best thinking doesn't happen on demand — it happens when your biology is ready for it.

The practical implication is simple: protect your peak for your hardest, most important work. Use your trough for email, admin, low-stakes decisions, routine tasks. Don't schedule a critical creative session at 3pm if 3pm is when you hit a wall.

Tell Edge your peak and trough windows — or let it learn from your patterns — and it will organize your calendar around them automatically. Your peak hours become a protected resource, not just another time slot.

## 8. What Open Loops are — and why they matter

**Preview:** That nagging feeling you can't shake? It has a name. And it's costing you more than you think.

**Body:**
An open loop is any commitment, task, or intention that lives in your head instead of on your calendar. The email you meant to send. The conversation you've been putting off. The decision you keep circling back to without resolving.

Psychologists call this the Zeigarnik effect: your brain holds unfinished tasks in active memory, quietly consuming cognitive resources in the background — even when you're trying to focus on something else. Open loops don't just feel stressful. They fragment attention and drain the mental energy you need for your most important work.

Edge surfaces your open loops every morning — things you mentioned on previous calls, patterns in your calendar, threads that haven't been closed. Not to add pressure, but to help you decide: act on it now, schedule it, or consciously set it aside. A loop you've chosen to defer is no longer an open loop. It's a decision.

The goal isn't an empty list. It's a clear head — so you can focus on what actually matters today.

## 9. How Edge reads your email — and what it ignores

**Preview:** Edge scans your inbox so you don't have to — but it's looking for something specific.

**Body:**
Edge doesn't read your email to summarize newsletters or keep tabs on your social life. It's looking for signals that matter to your focus: urgent threads, financial or legal items, replies from people you've reached out to, anything that might need to move up on your priority list.

When something important surfaces — a reply you've been waiting on, a financial notice, a time-sensitive thread — Edge factors it into your morning briefing and your focus recommendations. If a collector email lands overnight, it doesn't compete silently with your calendar. Edge sees it and weighs it.

What Edge ignores: promotions, newsletters, automated notifications, anything that isn't a real signal from a real person about something that matters.

The result is a version of inbox zero that doesn't require you to actually process your inbox. Edge handles the triage. You deal with what's real.

Your email data stays private — Edge reads signals, not content, and never stores the body of your emails beyond what's needed to surface the insight.

## 10. The cost of context-switching

**Preview:** Every time you switch tasks, you pay a tax. Most people don't know how high it is.

**Body:**
When you move from deep work to a Slack message to a quick email to back to deep work, it feels like multitasking. It isn't. It's sequential task-switching — and each switch carries a cognitive cost.

Research consistently shows it takes an average of 23 minutes to fully regain focus after an interruption. Which means a morning of five small interruptions doesn't just cost you the interruptions — it can cost you the morning.

The damage compounds when interruptions hit high-demand work. Answering a quick message during a creative or strategic session doesn't pause the work — it resets it. You come back to a colder problem, with less momentum and often less insight than you had before you left.

Edge protects against this in two ways. First, it builds your day with buffers — transitions between events, protected blocks where notifications have no business landing. Second, it identifies your highest-demand focus work and shields those windows from low-stakes interruptions.

The simplest rule: protect your deep work like a meeting you can't reschedule. Because that's exactly what it is.

## 11. Protecting recovery on a red day

**Preview:** A red day isn't a bad day. It's a signal — and how you respond to it determines tomorrow.

**Body:**
A red recovery score doesn't mean you're broken. It means your body spent more energy than it recovered, and it's asking for a different kind of day.

The instinct for most high performers is to push through. To treat a red day like a yellow one, or worse, to compensate with caffeine and willpower. This works — once, twice, maybe a week. Then it stops working and you wonder why you're exhausted.

The smarter move is to treat the red day as information. Your capacity is lower. Your cognitive performance is measurably affected — especially on tasks requiring complex judgment, creativity, or emotional regulation. This isn't weakness; it's physiology.

Edge responds to a red day by reshaping your calendar: moving high-demand blocks, protecting recovery time, front-loading lighter work. Not because ambition gets suspended, but because protecting today's recovery is how you unlock tomorrow's performance.

One well-managed red day doesn't set you back. A series of ignored red days is how burnout starts.

## 12. Designing your ideal week

**Preview:** Most people react to their week. High performers design it.

**Body:**
The difference between a week that feels productive and one that feels like you survived it often comes down to one thing: intentionality. Did you decide how the week would go — or did the week decide for you?

Designing your ideal week doesn't mean over-scheduling every hour. It means having a template: which kinds of work go where, which days are for deep focus vs. external meetings, where recovery is protected, and where your three areas of focus have guaranteed time.

A simple starting point: anchor your week around your focus areas first. Block time for each one before the reactive work fills in. Then layer in the fixed commitments (recurring meetings, obligations). What's left is your margin — protect some of it, because margin is where thinking happens.

Edge helps maintain this template day-to-day. When the week fills up in ways that crowd out your focus areas, Edge notices and proposes corrections. When a red day hits, it adapts the plan without abandoning it.

The goal isn't a perfect week. It's a week that, on balance, moved the things that matter. That's what the Edge Score tracks — and what the morning call helps you recalibrate every single day.
