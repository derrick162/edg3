# Edge — ICP Validation Interview Guides
_Per-ICP question sets for testing key hypotheses. Use in discovery calls with design-partner
candidates or early-access users. 20–30 minutes per interview. Last updated: June 18, 2026._

---

## How to use these guides

**Goal:** Validate (or invalidate) the key hypotheses for each ICP before investing in features
or messaging designed for them. You're not pitching — you're listening.

**Format:** Conversational, not a questionnaire. Pick 5–7 questions per section; don't ask all
of them. The best interviews feel like a great conversation about the person's work, not a form.

**The golden rule:** After every answer, ask "tell me more about that" or "what happened then?"
before moving on. The follow-up is where the real data lives.

**What you're looking for:** Specific, recent, emotional stories about calendar/focus/burnout
pain. Generic answers ("I'm pretty busy") are not useful. Specific ones ("last Tuesday I
missed my kid's recital because I couldn't leave a meeting I wasn't even needed in") are gold.

**After the interview:** Within 30 minutes, write down:
1. The one most surprising thing they said
2. The exact words they used to describe their problem (quote verbatim)
3. Whether this is a YES (strong ICP fit), MAYBE, or NO
4. The one hypothesis this interview confirmed or invalidated

## ICP 1 — ADHD Founder/Operator

_Beachhead ICP. Derrick IS this person. Validate that the product isn't just solving Derrick's
specific problem, but a broadly-shared class of ADHD executive pain._

### Hypothesis set
- **H1:** ADHD executives feel that calendar failures are their fault, not a tooling failure
- **H2:** Decision fatigue around "what should I work on right now" is daily and debilitating
- **H3:** Time blindness (overrunning tasks, missing transitions) is a bigger problem than task capture
- **H4:** They've tried every system and eventually abandoned each one
- **H5:** They'll trust an AI faster than neurotypical peers if it doesn't require setup

### Warm-up (3 minutes)
- Tell me about your week. How's it going so far?
- What does a typical day look like for you? Walk me through it.

### Problem depth (10 minutes)
- When you sit down to work in the morning, what's the first thing you do?
  - _Listen for: scrolling, email checking, delay in starting actual work_
- When do you feel most productive? What are the conditions?
- What gets in the way of that most often?
- Tell me about a week recently where things went sideways. What happened?
  - _Listen for: cascading missed commitments, context-switching spiral, energy crash_
- How do you figure out what to work on? Do you have a system?
  - _Listen for: "I try to..." (failed system), "I just sort of feel it out" (no system), or describing how often the system breaks_
- Have you ever missed something really important — a meeting, a deadline, a commitment — because of how your brain works? What happened?
  - _This is an ICP litmus: if they have a story here, they're in. If they say "not really," probe further or reconsider._

### Tool and system history (5 minutes)
- What have you tried to help with focus and calendar? What happened?
  - _Listen for: Notion, ClickUp, Reclaim, Motion, Sunsama, paper, apps on apps_
- What made you stop using [specific tool they mentioned]?
  - _"I just didn't keep up with it" = onboarding and maintenance friction. This is Edge's advantage._
- What do you actually use right now?

### Edge fit questions (5 minutes, after you've established the problem — NOT a pitch)
- If you could have someone who had read your calendar, knew your priorities, and had a strong opinion — call you every morning and tell you what to do with your day — would that be useful? Why or why not?
- What would make you trust something like that?
- What would make you not trust it?

### What a "yes" looks like
- They have at least one specific, emotional story about calendar/focus failure
- They describe having tried and abandoned multiple systems
- They express some version of "the problem is I can't get myself to do the thing I know I should do"
- When you describe the morning call concept, their reaction is curiosity or relief — not skepticism

### What a "no" looks like
- Their main complaint is "not enough hours in the day" rather than "I can't control the hours I have"
- They have a working system they're happy with
- They're skeptical of AI in a way that suggests high trust threshold (route to ICP2 or ICP4 instead)

## ICP 2 — Burnt-Out Senior Executive

_Harder ICP: higher trust threshold, longer sales cycle, less personal flexibility to experiment.
Validate that burnout is acute enough to drive genuine behavioral change._

### Hypothesis set
- **H1:** Calendar chaos is understood as the symptom; burnout is the underlying fear
- **H2:** They feel they've lost control of their calendar and don't know how to get it back
- **H3:** The real problem is strategic drift — they're doing the wrong things, not the right things slowly
- **H4:** A personal chief-of-staff framing resonates more than a productivity tool framing
- **H5:** Whoop recovery data integration would be a meaningful differentiator for this ICP

### Warm-up (3 minutes)
- How are you doing? How's the last few weeks been?
  - _Listen for: "I'm exhausted," "there's so much going on," "I'm behind." These are all ICP signals._
- What's taking up most of your time right now?

### Problem depth (10 minutes)
- Walk me through what your calendar looks like most weeks. How much of it is in your control?
- When was the last time you had a focused day — where you worked on what you intended to work on, with no major interruptions?
  - _If they struggle to remember, that's the data._
- How do you decide what's most important each week? What does that process look like?
- What would a week that went really well look like, versus the typical week? What's the gap?
- Tell me about a decision you made recently — either work or personal — where you think your energy or stress level affected the quality of your thinking.
  - _This surfaces burnout-cognitive-performance connection without you naming it_
- What's the thing that gets dropped when things get busy? For a lot of people it's the personal stuff — health, relationships, big-picture thinking. Is that true for you?

### Tool and system history (5 minutes)
- Do you have an EA or chief of staff? If yes, how much does that person take off your plate?
  - _If yes: what would make it even more helpful? If no: have you thought about it? What's stopped you?_
- What tools or approaches have you tried for calendar management?
- What would it actually take to make you change your morning routine?

### Edge fit questions (5 minutes)
- If you could get a clear, honest picture of where your time is going — and where it should go — every morning in 10 minutes, would you use it?
- What would make you trust that picture?
- Would knowing your recovery score (from Whoop or similar) as part of that picture change how you made decisions? How?

### What a "yes" looks like
- They express some version of "I've lost control" or "there's too much"
- They have a clear gap between what they intend to do and what they actually do
- They mention burnout, energy, or "I'm not at my best" without being prompted
- They're curious about the Whoop integration

### What a "no" looks like
- They're under-leveraged (light calendar, not much going on) — they don't have the problem yet
- They have a highly functional EA and the problem is largely solved
- They're in denial about burnout ("I'm fine, just busy") — won't engage with Edge's core framing
- Extreme AI skepticism: "I don't want an AI having access to my calendar"

## ICP 3 — Early-Stage Solo Founder

_Youngest, most time-strapped ICP. Fast to try, fast to abandon. Validate that the pain is acute
enough to sustain a daily habit without external pressure._

### Hypothesis set
- **H1:** Context-switching cost is their #1 productivity drain, not total hours
- **H2:** They have no system at all — the calendar is chaotic, not over-filled
- **H3:** Email signal (what they're missing) is a meaningful use case for this ICP
- **H4:** They're building something and burnout prevention isn't the frame — effectiveness is
- **H5:** The accountability loop (daily call = daily commitment) is the key habit-former

### Warm-up (3 minutes)
- Tell me about what you're building. Where are things at?
- What does your typical day look like?

### Problem depth (10 minutes)
- How do you decide what to work on each day?
- When you sit down to start work, how long does it take you to feel "in it"?
  - _If more than 20 minutes, that's context-switching friction — a core problem_
- What derails you most often? What pulls you out of flow?
- Tell me about the last time you worked on the wrong thing — something that felt productive but in retrospect wasn't the highest leverage.
- How many things are "in progress" right now — half-finished projects, open threads, things you've been meaning to get back to?
  - _"Open loops" language: if they say something like "way too many," they're in_
- Is there anything you're avoiding right now? Something important that you keep not doing?

### Email and signal (5 minutes)
- How do you handle email? What's your inbox like?
- Have you ever missed something important in email — a time-sensitive opportunity, a message you should have responded to faster?
- What would be useful: having someone scan your inbox and tell you "these three threads matter, everything else can wait"?

### Edge fit questions (5 minutes)
- If someone called you every morning — not to hassle you, but to be honest about where you're spending time and what you're avoiding — would that change your behavior?
- What would make you actually pick up every morning?

### What a "yes" looks like
- They have a clear pattern of context-switching and shallow work
- They can name something they're avoiding
- They have open-loop anxiety (lots of things "in progress")
- They're open to the morning call concept immediately — it fits their style

### What a "no" looks like
- They have great calendar discipline already
- They're in deep execution mode (heads-down building) and don't want interruption
- They're too early/pre-product (lifestyle problems aren't salient yet)
- The morning call format doesn't fit (very early riser, very late starter, unpredictable schedule)

## ICP 4 — VC-Backed Founder (Series A–B)

_Highest potential user but hardest to hold. Validate that strategic drift + board pressure creates
acute-enough pain to sustain a daily tool habit amid a chaotic schedule._

### Hypothesis set
- **H1:** Strategic drift — doing IC work instead of founder work — is the core pain
- **H2:** They feel accountable to others (investors, board) but not to themselves in a useful way
- **H3:** Edge Score as a new metric resonates because they're metrics-fluent
- **H4:** Calendar hygiene (removing the wrong meetings) matters as much as adding the right ones
- **H5:** Runway and time-to-next-milestone create a time-pressure frame that makes Edge's ROI obvious

### Warm-up (3 minutes)
- Where are things at with the business? What's the biggest challenge right now?
- How are you spending most of your time?

### Problem depth (10 minutes)
- When you look back at last week, what did you actually spend most of your time on?
  - _Then: "Was that the highest-leverage thing you could have been doing?"_
- What percentage of your time is in meetings versus doing actual work? How does that feel?
- When was the last time you had a full day to think — not execute, not meet, just think?
- Tell me about a week where you were clearly in the wrong zone — doing IC work, being pulled into execution when you should have been strategic. What happened?
- How do you make decisions about what's on your calendar? Who has access? What gets cut?
- What does your board or investors think is the most important thing you should be working on right now? Do you agree? Are you actually spending time on it?

### Trust and data (5 minutes)
- How do you track your own effectiveness? Do you have any kind of personal metrics?
  - _"Not really" is fine — but if they say yes, ask what and why_
- Would seeing a number that summarized your week — how focused you were, how much calendar time went to the right things — be useful or annoying?
- What would you need to believe about how that number was calculated to trust it?

### Edge fit questions (5 minutes)
- What would you need to see in the first week for you to keep using something like this?
- How do your current tools (CoS, EA, whatever) fall short? What's the gap?

### What a "yes" looks like
- They have a clear sense of what "founder-mode work" looks like and they're not doing enough of it
- They express frustration about being in execution vs. strategy
- They're metrics-fluent and curious about a "productivity metric"
- They mention runway, board, or time pressure

### What a "no" looks like
- The company is doing really well and they're energized — no acute pain
- They have a strong operational team doing the execution, so they're not in the IC trap
- They want things their current EA handles (travel, scheduling, admin) — Edge isn't an EA replacement for logistics
- They don't have calendar discipline problems — they have hiring/fundraising/product problems (out of scope)

## Cross-ICP questions (use when you want a quick ICP read)

Use these 3 questions to quickly identify which ICP bucket someone falls into before going deep.

1. **"Walk me through a day in your life last week."**
   - Heavy on meetings + reactive = ICP 2 or 4
   - Context-switching chaos = ICP 1 or 3
   - Mentions energy/exhaustion without being asked = ICP 2

2. **"What's the most expensive mistake you made with your time in the last month?"**
   - "I worked on the wrong thing" = ICP 1, 3, or 4
   - "I said yes to too many things" = ICP 2
   - Blank stare / "I don't think I made a big mistake" = probably not a strong fit

3. **"If Edge didn't exist, what would be the next-best alternative?"**
   - "I'd hire an EA" = ICP 2 or 4 (premium market, high value)
   - "I'd try yet another app" = ICP 1 or 3 (tool-switching pattern = we can win on simplicity)
   - "I don't know, I've just been living with it" = high pain, low discovery = hot lead
