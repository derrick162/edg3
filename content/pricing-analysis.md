## The pricing question

Pricing does three things simultaneously:
1. **Filters the right users in** — a price point signals which category you're in
2. **Filters the wrong users out** — low price attracts users who won't pay more later
3. **Signals value** — users who pay more use tools more seriously and give better feedback

The biggest pricing mistake for Edge would be anchoring to consumer productivity apps ($9.99–$19.99).
That's the Todoist/Notion tier. Edge isn't in that category. Edge is closer to a human
assistant — and should be priced accordingly.

---

## The reference frame that matters

| Alternative | Monthly cost | Edge's advantage |
|---|---|---|
| Executive assistant (junior) | $3,000–$6,000/month | Edge = 1/50th the cost; calls you at 7 AM |
| Business coach | $800–$2,000/month | Edge calls you daily; coach calls weekly |
| Reclaim / Motion | $10–$49/month | Edge adds intent understanding + voice + energy |

The framing should be: **Edge replaces the accountability and focus-clarity function of a coach
or EA, at a fraction of the cost.** Not "Edge is a premium productivity app."

---

## Option A — $49/month

**What it signals:** Accessible, but clearly above the $9.99 consumer tier. Signals a
professional tool without being gatekeeping.

**Who it works for:**
- ICP 1 (ADHD Founder/Operator): most accessible; ADHD founders are often cash-constrained
  early-stage; $49 removes money as a reason not to try
- ICP 3 (Solo Founder): same — early-stage founders watch every dollar; $49 is serious but not painful

**Revenue math (very rough):**
- 100 users → $4,900/month ($58,800 ARR)
- 1,000 users → $49,000/month ($588,000 ARR)

**Risks:**
- Some ICP 2 and ICP 4 users may perceive $49 as "consumer tier" — they expect to pay more for
  something they take seriously
- At $49, churn from low-commitment users is more painful (lower LTV means higher CAC tolerance)
- Hard to raise later — moving from $49 to $79 feels like a 60% increase

**Verdict:** The floor. A good price for wave 1 design partners (run them free, charge at $49
for the broader waitlist). May need to increase before wave 2.

---

## Option B — $69/month

**What it signals:** Mid-tier professional tool. Comfortable for most ICP 1–4. Clearly not a
consumer app. Feels like "I'm serious about this."

**Who it works for:**
- Broadest ICP fit: accessible enough for ADHD founders and solo founders, credible enough for
  senior execs and VC-backed founders
- The "I'd pay that without thinking too hard" tier for most knowledge workers earning $100K+
- Gives room to discount for annual billing ($55–60/month annual = meaningful savings without
  destroying unit economics)

**Revenue math:**
- 100 users → $6,900/month ($82,800 ARR)
- 1,000 users → $69,000/month ($828,000 ARR)

**Annual billing option:** $690/year ($57.50/month equivalent) — a ~17% discount for commitment.
At $690/year, users are meaningfully more engaged (they've paid up front) and churn risk drops.

**Risks:**
- Slightly harder initial conversion vs. $49 (but likely marginal)
- At $69, users have higher expectations for polish — the product needs to deliver before raising
  to this tier broadly

**Verdict:** Recommended anchor price. Not too low to feel consumer; not too high to deter
the ADHD founder beachhead. Gives room for annual billing and an eventual professional tier.

---

## Option C — $99/month (with a lower entry tier)

**What it signals:** Serious professional tool. "I pay $99/month for things I use daily." This
is the Superhuman, Loom, Figma tier.

**Who it works for:**
- ICP 2 (Burnt-Out Senior Executive) and ICP 4 (VC-Backed Founder): at $99/month, they're
  still paying less than one hour of an EA. The value prop is clear.
- Users who anchor quality to price: if it costs $99, "it must be good"

**Revenue math:**
- 100 users → $9,900/month ($118,800 ARR)
- 1,000 users → $99,000/month ($1.2M ARR)

**The two-tier structure:**
- $49/month — **Focus plan**: morning call, Edge Score, calendar reshape, basic memory
- $99/month — **Edge plan**: everything + email signal (inbox reading), Whoop integration,
  advanced analytics, priority derivation

**Why two tiers might work:**
- Lets price-sensitive ICP 1 and ICP 3 start lower and upgrade
- Creates a natural upsell moment (connected Gmail, connected Whoop → upgrade to unlock)
- The $99 tier frames Edge as enterprise-quality without requiring an enterprise sales motion

**Risks:**
- Two tiers adds product complexity before product-market fit is confirmed
- Feature gating (email, Whoop behind $99) may create resentment from users who feel the core
  product is being withheld
- Building two tiers = two support models, two sets of user expectations

**Verdict:** Interesting for wave 2, but premature for wave 1. Too much complexity before you
know which features drive retention. Table for the wave-2 pricing conversation.

## Special cases

### Design partners (wave 1)
**Recommendation: free.** Design partners are doing you a favor. They're giving time, attention,
and feedback. Charging them creates friction and sets up the relationship wrong. Run wave 1 free,
explicitly ("You're one of the first 5 people with access — no charge, ever. I'll treat this as
our exchange for your feedback.").

If they insist on paying: accept $1/month or a symbolic amount. It creates commitment without
being extractive.

### Annual billing
Add annual billing before wave 2. Suggested: ~15–17% discount off monthly. At $69/month:
$690/year ($57.50/month equivalent). At $99/month: $990/year ($82.50/month equivalent).

Annual billing improves cash flow, reduces churn, and signals commitment from users. SaaS
businesses with high annual billing ratios are dramatically more predictable.

### Founding member pricing
Consider: "Founding members" who join within the first 90 days lock in their monthly rate
forever. This is a powerful conversion lever ("act now or pay more later") and rewards early
adopters without creating ongoing pricing complexity.

At $69/month: founding members lock at $69, future price is $89 or $99. This creates urgency
without deception — and gives you runway to raise prices as the product matures.

## What to decide before wave 2

1. **What do wave-1 design partners pay?** (Recommendation: free)
2. **What does wave-2 open at?** ($49, $69, or $99?)
3. **Is there annual billing?** (Recommendation: yes, ~15% discount)
4. **Is there a founding-member lock?** (Recommendation: yes, for first 90 days)
5. **Is there a free trial?** (Recommendation: 7-day trial or free first week — low enough risk
   to try, high enough commitment to see the product work)

---

## The framing to give users

Don't lead with the monthly price. Lead with the value comparison:

> "A good business coach costs $200/hour. Edge calls you every morning for less than $3/day."

> "A junior EA costs $3,000/month. Edge costs 2% of that."

> "Netflix is $18/month for entertainment. Edge is $69/month for your focus and your time —
> what's worth more?"
